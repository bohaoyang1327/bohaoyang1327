`SIMAX` (Scalable and Interpretable Framework for Multi-Fidelity and Annotated Clinician-Patient Dialogue Simulation) generates controlled and reproducible clinical dialogue data with reference behavioral annotations. It was designed to support the development and evaluation of communication-coding systems when real-world conversations and human-coded labels are difficult to obtain at scale.

The framework creates clinician-patient dialogues from predefined clinical scenarios, personas, voice and accent conditions, and target communication behaviors. It uses two complementary codebooks: the **Global Codebook** for overall communication quality and the **WISER Codebook** for specific, countable behaviors.

SIMAX generated **3,388 simulated dialogues** spanning three specialties, multiple visit stages, persona characteristics, and accent conditions. Evaluation combined automated speech-quality and transcription metrics, human ratings of naturalness and clinical realism, and downstream testing with a communication-coding system. The results show how controlled synthetic dialogues can reveal whether coding systems respond appropriately to targeted behaviors and where their sensitivity remains limited.
